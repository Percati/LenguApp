# LenguApp · quick guide for testers (version 0.4.5)

**What it is.** An app to practise a language one week at a time (German or English, A2 to C2). **It is not a course:** it's for consolidating what you already learned in one. It works with no internet, no account and no data collection. It's in testing, and your feedback helps a lot.

## This is on purpose (not a bug)
| What you see | Why |
|---|---|
| Only A2 and B1 can be translated into your language | At B2, C1 and C2 the sheet is always in the language you're learning. Vocabulary and expressions are translated, in the Library and in Saved. |
| Words in another language between « » | Any fragment in a language other than the surrounding text goes in angle quotes. If you see one unmarked, that **is** a bug: tell us. |
| A skill or a topic comes back later | On purpose: skills reappear several times a year at a different depth. |
| Special Review weeks | Four times a year there's a review week, in a different format. |
| The weekend challenge only appears on Saturday and Sunday | It's speaking out loud, not repeating the mission. It comes in the language you're learning (or translated, with the A2/B1 switch). It starts switched off and saves nothing. |
| «About» is at the end of Settings | The main screen stays free for the content. |
| Welcome and tutorial | The first time you pick language and level; each section then shows a walkthrough that dims everything except the thing it's explaining. In two steps you can tap what is lit up, and the walkthrough never moves on by itself. «Skip» skips them. You can see it all again from Settings. |
| Saved, and import/export | Saved shows the same as the Library. You can export to Anki or make a backup and import it later; you choose the file yourself at that moment. |
| Skills in the Library | A read-only list: tap a skill to open its week. Sorted by title or by week, and only the weeks that have already come round (a previous year, in full). |
| Verbs in the Library | Lists the verbs of the language, from every level; under each one, in German, the case or preposition it takes (von + Dat) and, in English, whether the phrasal verb is separable. It also includes reference verbs (A1–A2) that appear in no week: with a translation, without a star. The phrases they're used in appear too (die Frage stellen); a verb that only exists inside phrases (stellen) has no star. You filter by class, and the search box at the top also looks at translations and phrases: «Frage» finds stellen. |
| No stats, streaks or progress | By design. The app only stores your stars (Saved) and your settings. |
| The voice-conversation prompt for an AI exists only in English B2 and C1 | It's a trial. The prompt for an AI to correct your writing is there at every level. |
| The AI prompts are always in the language you're learning | So the AI answers in that language, even if the app is in yours. |
| The PDF sheet comes out in the language you're learning, on a single page | It's to show to whoever teaches you that language. |
| German has no «ß» | Swiss spelling is used: «ss». |
| Spanish is neutral and uses «tú» | «Usted» only appears in phrases from formal situations, which is the thing being taught. |
| Audio works in German and in English | Tap the speaker next to a word or expression (in the examples, only some have it): a synthetic voice. In Settings you choose the speed (60–100 %). Both voices' licences are under «About». |
| There's content for 2026 (from week 41) and for 2027 | The rest will be added over time. |
| You can choose the colours and the type | Settings has four themes (Paper, Terracotta, Sea, Graphite) and, on Android 12 or later, one that follows your wallpaper. Also three typefaces: the system one, Reading and Clear. |
| Icon and splash screen | Still provisional: they're being redesigned. The splash screen has no logo yet. |

## We do want to hear about
Bugs in the app or in the texts, words in another language without « », translations that sound odd or wrong (especially French, Italian and Portuguese, which a native speaker hasn't reviewed yet), and anything that isn't clear.

## How to tell us
Tell us: 1) which screen you were on, 2) which language and level you were learning, 3) which language the app was in, 4) what you expected and what happened, and 5) a screenshot if you can. The version is shown in «About».
